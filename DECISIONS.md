## Phase 2a — pipeline at full scale (2026-07-31)

- 2026-07-31 — The build pipeline lives in `pipeline/build/` with shared code in `pipeline/lib/`; `pipeline/spike/` is left untouched as the historical record. Raw downloads moved from `pipeline/spike/raw/` to `pipeline/raw/`, manifest included. — Moving the manifest preserves the recorded download dates and sha256s, so `DATA_SOURCES.md` provenance stays truthful without re-downloading 150 MB. The spike scripts would re-download if ever re-run; their committed outputs are what matters as the record.
- 2026-07-31 — The pipeline imports `normalize` from `src/lib/normalize.js` instead of keeping its own copy. — The pipeline builds the index the app searches; two copies of the normalizer would eventually disagree about ñ, and the failure would look like "search is broken" rather than "two functions drifted".
- 2026-07-31 — Big intermediates are JSONL, not one JSON object (`raw/_forms-search.jsonl`, `_forms-exact.jsonl`). — At ~1.2 M rows the single-object form has to be parsed in full before the next script can do one lookup; streaming keeps each step's memory proportional to what it actually keeps.
- 2026-07-31 — Parts of speech excluded from the dictionary: `character`, `punct`, `symbol`, `prefix`, `suffix`, `infix`, `interfix`, `romanization` (1,509 records). Proper nouns (`name`) are KEPT. — Affixes and single characters are not words a learner looks up; proper nouns are, and frequency ranking decides whether any earns a slot (exactly 0 did).
- 2026-07-31 — **Ambiguity policy refined to citation-form-first + proportional-to-evidence.** Ambiguous *inflected* tokens are now split in proportion to each candidate's score from *unambiguous* tokens, instead of evenly. — Full scale exposed what the spike's 186-record sample could not: rare words riding common ones' inflections. `podar` (to prune) reached rank 321 on *poder*'s "podamos", archaic `seer` reached 241 on *ser*'s "sea", and the neologisms `amigx`/`chicx`/`niñe` reached the top 700 on amigo/chico/niño's plurals. Proportional allocation drops them to 6468 / 4984 / 33155 while *raising* real verbs whose infinitive is rare (preocupar 269→257, soler 766→677) — their evidence comes from unambiguous forms like "preocupa".
- 2026-07-31 — Citation-form ambiguity still splits EVENLY; only inflected ambiguity is evidence-weighted. — Weighting it is actively wrong: invariable words have no inflections, so a conjunction's evidence score is structurally zero. Weighting citation forms sent `pero` (the conjunction, one of the commonest words in Spanish) from rank 52 to 5484, because `pero` the noun earns evidence from its plural "peros". Same failure for `adiós`. Evidence-weighting can answer "which lemma does this inflected form belong to"; it cannot answer "which part of speech did the speaker mean".
- 2026-07-31 — Only lemmas with a real kaikki lemma record are ranked (`raw/_lemma-keys.json` from step 02). — Inflection stubs sometimes name a target that has no record of its own; the English `your` arrived that way and reached rank 74, silently spending a top-10k slot on a lemma that can never become an entry. Removing phantoms dropped the ranked list from 70,850 to 61,236.
- 2026-07-31 — Senses carry a `labels[]` array (colloquial, slang, vulgar, reflexive, figuratively…) alongside `regionLabels[]`. — A learner needs to know that *güey* is vulgar slang and that a verb is reflexive; this is the same "adapt details, keep the shape" latitude §7 allows. kaikki's remaining tag vocabulary is inflection bookkeeping and stays out of the shipped data.
- 2026-07-31 — Unmarked senses sort before Spain-only senses, after Mexico and Latin America. — §3 asks for Mexico first; an unlabelled sense is general Spanish and is more useful to this owner than a Spain-only one.
- 2026-07-31 — Examples are chosen by score (citation form present, length near 45 characters) rather than "shortest wins" as in the spike. — Shortest-wins favours fragments; a sentence showing the word in its dictionary form teaches more. 83.5% of the 28,210 attached examples show the citation form; coverage is 95.9% of entries.

## Phase 2b — conjugations (2026-07-31)

- 2026-07-31 — **Conjugations for the 1,253 verbs Jehle lacks are EXTRACTED from kaikki's tagged `forms[]`, not generated from orthographic rules.** — Every Spanish verb record in kaikki already carries a fully tagged conjugation table written by Wiktionary editors who handled the stem changes, the `-gar`/`-car`/`-zar` spelling rules and every irregular. Extraction inherits all of that; the rule-based generator scoped in the spike report would have had to re-earn it. Verb coverage went from 30.7% to **99.3%** (1,795 of 1,807).
- 2026-07-31 — The extractor is validated cell by cell against Jehle on the 554 verbs both sources cover: **99.83% agreement across 57,580 cells**, gate was 99.5%. — This is the argument for trusting extraction, and no hand-written conjugator could have offered it. `pipeline/build/06-conjugate.mjs` exits non-zero below the gate, so a future dataset refresh cannot silently degrade.
- 2026-07-31 — Where the two sources disagree, **kaikki is the more accurate one**. — `criáis`→`criais` and `frió`→`frio` are the 2010 RAE spelling reform (Jehle predates it); `gradúéis` in Jehle carries two accents, which Spanish orthography does not allow; `arrepentáis` should be `arrepintáis`. Recorded because it bears on the licensing question below.
- 2026-07-31 — Negative imperatives extracted from kaikki are prefixed with "no " to match Jehle's convention. — kaikki stores the bare subjunctive ("abandones"); the negative imperative does not exist without its "no", and normalizing is what made the two sources comparable at all (it accounted for 2,729 of the original 2,746 disagreements).
- 2026-07-31 — Voseo forms (`vos-form`, `with-vos`) are skipped rather than written into the tú cell. — This is a Mexico-leaning notebook (§3) and vos would silently replace a form the owner will actually hear with one they will not.
- 2026-07-31 — The eight perfect tenses are composed as haber + past participle for **both** sources, rather than using Jehle's stored compound rows. — They are mechanically derivable, the composition agrees with Jehle on 99.8% of cells, and some Jehle rows are corrupt: `cepillar`'s past perfect reads "cepillía cepillado" where it should read "había cepillado". One code path means neither source can ship a mangled auxiliary.
- 2026-07-31 — Reflexive verbs put the pronoun before the auxiliary ("me he arrepentido"), detected from the verb's own conjugated forms rather than from a `-se` ending. — Spelling is not the signal: *coser* ends in -se and is not reflexive. Found by validating the composed perfects against Jehle, which is the reason perfects are validated at all.
- 2026-07-31 — Jehle cells that are empty or malformed are repaired from the kaikki table (158 cells). — `doler`'s negative imperatives are the literal string "no " with no verb after it. This repairs a defect; it does not change which source is primary.
- 2026-07-31 — Jehle remains the primary source for the 554 verbs it covers, as brief §4 specifies. — **Open question for the owner:** since kaikki matches Jehle to 99.83% and is more accurate where they differ, Jehle is now technically redundant. Dropping it would remove the CC BY-NC-SA **noncommercial** restriction from the entire dataset — the one licence the spike report flagged as constraining "what this app may ever become". Not acted on, because §4 locks Jehle in and this is a licensing decision, not a technical one.
- 2026-07-31 — 12 verbs still have no conjugation table (`había`, `persona`, `escucho`, `ponerle`, `hope`…). — These are kaikki records tagged `verb` that are not verb lemmas: inflected forms, a noun, and one English word. 0.1% of entries, each with a real gloss, so they are left rather than filtered by a rule that might catch genuine rare verbs.

## Phase 2c — packaging (2026-07-31)

- 2026-07-31 — The bundle ships as plain JSON chunks under `public/dict/<version>/`, committed to the repo and deployed with the app. — GitHub Pages gzips text over the wire, so 22.6 MB raw arrives as 3.3 MB with no custom compression code. Generating them in CI is impractical: the pipeline needs 150 MB of source downloads.
- 2026-07-31 — The form and English indexes are **sharded by first two letters** (326 + 330 shards) rather than stored one row per term. — Install time is dominated by IndexedDB writes: 656 shard rows land in seconds where 239,000 individual rows would take minutes, and a lookup still reads exactly one small row. The shard also doubles as a prefix bucket, which is what makes "saca" → *sacar* possible without a second index.
- 2026-07-31 — Three measured size cuts took the bundle from 4.0 MB to 3.3 MB, under the 3.5 MB budget: example licence + URL strings are constants stored once in the manifest (301 KB); `normalizedLemma` is not stored because the form index already maps it (208 KB); the eight perfect tenses are not stored because they are haber + participle without exception (322 KB). — Each was measured before being cut, not guessed. Every example still displays its ID, contributor, licence and link, which is what §4 requires the reader to see.
- 2026-07-31 — Perfect-tense composition lives in `src/lib/conjugation.js`, which the **pipeline imports**. — Same reasoning as `normalize`: the pipeline writes these tables and the app reads them, so one implementation and one test suite. The build validates the shared function against Jehle, so the app's rendering is covered by that 99.83% number too.
- 2026-07-31 — The per-cell `collapsed: true` flag is gone from conjugation data. — Which slots collapse is a display rule (§3) and belongs in the component; as data it was a constant repeated on ~106,000 cells.
- 2026-07-31 — `verify-package.mjs` round-trips the shipped chunks: verifies every sha256, reassembles the stores as the installer will, and answers the §12 searches from them. — Everything upstream tests the pipeline's intermediates; this tests what actually ships.

## Phase 2d — reference store and the §11 download (2026-07-31)

- 2026-07-31 — Reference data lives in **its own Dexie databases**, not in new tables of `mi-cuaderno`. — §5 says rebuilding the dictionary must never touch personal data. Separate databases make that structural rather than a rule to remember: no reference migration can see a personal record, and vice versa.
- 2026-07-31 — There are **two** reference databases, `mi-cuaderno-ref-a` and `-b`, with a localStorage pointer naming the live one. — §11 requires atomic version swaps and an interrupted download that leaves the previous version usable. An install writes into the inactive database; flipping one string is the entire commit, so there is no moment where the app reads a half-written dataset.
- 2026-07-31 — The active-slot pointer is localStorage, not IndexedDB. — `activeDb()` is called by every dictionary read, and making it async would infect every call site. Both desync directions degrade safely: a lost pointer offers a re-download, and a lost database reads as "not installed".
- 2026-07-31 — Each chunk's sha256 is verified before it is written, and each chunk is applied in one transaction. — A truncated download should fail loudly rather than become a dictionary with a hole in it that surfaces months later as a word mysteriously not being found.
- 2026-07-31 — Install progress is recorded per chunk, so an interrupted download resumes rather than restarting — but only when the pending install is the same `datasetVersion`. — Resuming into a slot holding a different version's rows would mix two datasets.
- 2026-07-31 — The service worker's `globIgnores` excludes `dict/**`, stated explicitly rather than relying on the default glob. — The dictionary is an explicit owner-initiated download that belongs in IndexedDB (§11); a Workbox default change must not silently start precaching 22 MB on first visit. Verified: the production build precaches 383 KB.
- 2026-07-31 — The About screen renders the §4 attribution from the **installed manifest**, not from a hard-coded list. — It can then never describe a different dataset than the one actually on the phone.
- 2026-07-31 — `localStorage` is polyfilled in `src/test/setup.js`. — The node test environment lacks it; polyfilling the environment is better than reshaping the app around the test runner.

## Phase 2e — two-layer search and dictionary UI (2026-07-31)

- 2026-07-31 — **A dictionary result whose entry is attached to a personal item is replaced by that item**, carrying the dictionary's match reason. — This is the seam (§5) paying off in both directions. It stops *sacar* appearing twice, once as the owner's word and once as the dictionary's; and it lets the reference layer find the owner's own words through inflections the personal layer cannot resolve — searching "madrugué" surfaces their note on *madrugar*, labelled "form of madrugar". A test written against the weaker behaviour (drop the duplicate, show nothing extra) is what exposed the missed opportunity.
- 2026-07-31 — Within one §8 tier, personal items rank above dictionary entries; tiers still decide first. — It is the owner's notebook, so their note on a word beats the dictionary's definition of it — but an exact dictionary match still outranks a personal page that merely mentions the word in its body, because tier 1 beats tier 6.
- 2026-07-31 — Prefix search matches only the **lemma**, not every form beginning with the query. — The shard is keyed on the first two letters, so it already holds every candidate and no second index is needed; but without the lemma filter, typing "sac" would bury *sacar* under its own conjugations. `saca` finds *sacar*; `saq` finds nothing, because "saque" is an inflection and not a word the owner was typing the start of.
- 2026-07-31 — English lookup **intersects** the postings of every query word rather than unioning them. — "take out" must find *sacar*, not everything meaning "take" or everything meaning "out". A gloss containing the whole phrase sorts above one containing the words scattered.
- 2026-07-31 — `search_miss` is logged against the **combined** result count, and suppressed while the dictionary query is in flight. — §7 wants "words I couldn't find". A word the dictionary answers is not one of them, and a slow IndexedDB read must not turn a found word into a miss on a slower phone. Verified in the browser: "chamba" logs nothing, "zzqxwv" logs exactly one.
- 2026-07-31 — Dictionary results are suppressed when the owner filters to *páginas* or to a tag. — Those filters mean "look through my own things"; reference words would be noise, and a tag filter cannot apply to an entry that has no tags.
- 2026-07-31 — Opening a dictionary entry logs a `view` event under its `dict:` key, through the same session window as a personal item. — §1 promises lookup history, and Phase 3's review queue will read it. `deriveItemState` already ignores events whose key is not a surviving personal item, so statistics were unaffected without changing them.
- 2026-07-31 — Dictionary search is debounced 140 ms and guarded against out-of-order replies. — The reference layer is asynchronous; without the guard a slow query for "sac" can overwrite the results for "sacar" typed after it.
- 2026-07-31 — Perfect tenses and vosotros are both collapsed behind toggles on the conjugation table. — §3 asks for vosotros collapsed; the perfect tenses are a further eighteen rows, and a learner wants the present before the future perfect subjunctive.
